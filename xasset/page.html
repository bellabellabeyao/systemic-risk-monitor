Liquidity escalation ladder

Four layers, from slow-moving to fast. The highest level reached today is outlined.

Level 0 · Crowded-position unwindPrice signal only: the 10-day liquidity shock is in its top 10%, or there were ≥ 2 days in the last 10 with equities down, Treasuries sold and gold down.
Level 1 · Market liquidity deterioratingMOVE or OFR safe-asset stress is in its worst 10% (or, from the last Bloomberg check, the UST liquidity index).
Level 2 · Funding stressSOFR 99th percentile ≥ 15bp over IORB, or SOFR ≥ 8bp over IORB, or OFR funding stress in its tightest 5% (or, from the last Bloomberg check, a sudden EUR/JPY basis widening).
Level 3 · Safe assets failingLevels 1 and 2 both on, plus ≥ 3 days in the last 10 with Treasuries sold alongside risk assets (or the liquidity shock in its top 2%).

Band: light grey = Level 0, light amber = Level 1, amber = Level 2, red = Level 3. Line = 10-day liquidity shock sum.

Method, shock definitions, data and limits

Model. Daily changes in 13 variables: S&P 500, semis (SOX, net of its rolling one-year beta to the S&P), 2y UST yield, 30y–2y slope, 10y real yield (TIPS), 10y breakeven, the dollar (DXY), USDJPY, USDCNH, gold, Brent, copper and a HY spread proxy. Sign restrictions identify ten shocks; the remaining three are 'other / unidentified'. Rotations are drawn as in Rubio-Ramírez, Waggoner and Zha (2010) and accepted only if every named shock meets all of its signs; no penalty function is used. Each estimate uses a 2-year rolling window, re-estimated every 21 trading days, starting in 2012. Contributions are averaged over accepted rotations (capped at 300 per window), so they add up exactly to the actual move. The design follows Jarociński & Karadi (2020) for monetary vs information shocks, Cieslak & Pang (2021) for using the curve to separate monetary from term-premium shocks, Brandt et al. (ECB WP 2560, 2021) for adding foreign and global shocks, and Ready (2018) for oil.

What each shock means. Every shock is a surprise relative to what was priced, not a level. Positive values push in the direction named; negative values are the opposite (e.g. a negative monetary shock is a dovish surprise).

  • Growth: better US news. Equities, 2y yields and breakevens up, HY spreads tighter.
  • Monetary (hawkish): the Fed path is repriced higher for reasons other than better growth. 2y and real yields up, curve flatter, breakevens down, dollar up, equities down.
  • Risk-off: classic flight to safety. Equities down, yields down, gold, dollar and yen up, HY wider.
  • Liquidity / deleveraging: a dash for cash. Equities and gold sold, long end sold while bills are bid (2y down, curve steeper, real yields up), breakevens down, dollar up, HY wider.
  • US risk premium: 'sell America'. Equities down, real yields up, dollar down, gold up.
  • Term premium: the long end reprices on supply or fiscal risk. Curve steeper with 2y also up, real yields up, equities and gold down.
  • Oil supply: oil up, breakevens up, equities down.
  • AI / tech: semis outperform their usual market beta and equities rise.
  • Global (China) growth: copper and oil up, yuan and other currencies stronger against the dollar, equities up.
  • Yen / carry: yen strengthens and the dollar weakens while equities fall, as in carry-trade unwinds.

Sign restrictions. The full table of signs that define each shock is in the appendix below.

Data. Yields are the US Treasury's daily par nominal and real (TIPS) curves, published the same evening; the breakeven is 10y nominal minus 10y real, as in FRED's T10YIE. Market prices are Yahoo Finance daily closes: S&P 500, SOX, DXY, USDJPY, USDCNH (onshore USDCNY when Yahoo has no offshore series; the two move almost one for one), COMEX gold and copper, ICE Brent. FRED now carries only three years of ICE BofA HY spreads, so the HY variable is a proxy: the return of HYG minus that of IEI (similar-duration Treasuries), divided by a spread duration of 3.5, in bp of spread. Funding uses New York Fed SOFR and its 99th percentile, FRED IORB/IOER and NFCI, and the OFR Financial Stress Index. Two inputs have no public source and come from occasional Bloomberg checks: the UST liquidity index (GVLQUSD, a curve-fitting-error measure in the spirit of Hu, Pan and Wang, 2013) and the 3-month EUR and JPY cross-currency bases (computed from covered interest parity with forward points and SOFR, €STR and TONA OIS). They are shown as percentiles with their date and count in the ladder for 7 days.

Limits. Sign restrictions identify a range of answers; the numbers shown are averages, not exact values. More shocks spread each move across more sources, so single-shock contributions are smaller than in a simpler model. Fingerprints shift across regimes, hence the rolling window. Public closes are not taken at the same moment, so single days are noisier than windows; see the accuracy table. The 2013 taper shows as US risk premium and monetary in roughly equal parts. The September 2024 China stimulus registers only a small global-growth shock, because semis and the yen moved at the same time. Positive AI / tech days include broad crisis rebounds, when semis rise more than their usual beta; the negative side (e.g. DeepSeek) is cleaner.

Appendix: what defines each shock

Each shock is identified only by the direction it must push these assets on the same day: + up, − down, blank = left free for the model to estimate. For USDJPY and USDCNH, − means the yen or yuan strengthens; for HY, + means spreads widen. Any shock can also run in reverse on a given day (a negative monetary shock is a dovish surprise). The three 'other / unidentified' shocks have no signs.
